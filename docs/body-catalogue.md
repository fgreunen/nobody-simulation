# Body catalogue and future boundaries

Body kind, descriptive subtype and orbital role are distinct (see [glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)). Only pre-main-sequence and main-sequence stars are generated. A simplified merger remnant is an explicit runtime exception. All stars gravitate; background decoration does not. No new category framework or unimplemented numerical models are implied by this catalogue.

| Kind | Meaningful inputs / dependencies | Units and supported ranges in this project | Generation / provenance / limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-main-sequence star | Mass, age, composition; contracting stellar interior determines R, T, L and stage together | Initial mass 0.08–4.5 M☉, age fixed 125 Myr; kind occupies the low-mass part according to model phase; R☉, K, L☉ | Low-mass-weighted Kroupa-like sampling; BHAC15 + MIST anchors. No independent radius/temperature draws, no age evolution. |
| Main-sequence star | Mass, age, composition; core hydrogen-burning model determines R, T, L | Same scene mass bounds and age; MIST stage selects this subset rather than a universal mass boundary | Same continuous sampling; solar-composition nonrotating MIST. Scoped upper mass 4.5 excludes the evolved turnoff. |
| Evolved star | Initial/current mass, age, metallicity, evolutionary stage, core/envelope structure, mass loss | No implemented ranges; future R☉, K, L☉ and M☉ | No generator. Requires a phase-aware isochrone/track including nonmonotonic and short-lived stages; cannot extend the current single stellar branch blindly. |
| White dwarf | Remnant mass, core/atmosphere composition, cooling age; cooling models determine radius, temperature and luminosity | Unsupported; future M☉, R☉, K, L☉ and years | No distribution. Requires progenitor-to-remnant relation plus published cooling tracks, not a main-sequence radius law. |
| Neutron star | Gravitational mass, equation of state, spin, magnetic field, cooling age | Unsupported; future M☉, km, s, magnetic-field units and K | No distribution. Radius and cooling require specialist models; relativistic regime outside this solver's collision approximation. |
| Black hole | Mass, spin and charge assumptions; horizon and accretion environment are distinct | Unsupported; future M☉, dimensionless spin, horizon km | No distribution. A horizon is not a stellar surface and has no ordinary stellar surface temperature or photospheric luminosity. Accretion emission requires a separate model. |
| Brown dwarf | Mass, cooling age, composition; substellar thermal history determines R, T, L | Unsupported; future Jupiter or solar masses/radii, K, L☉ | No generator. Hydrogen-burning boundaries depend on composition, not a universal single catalogue cutoff. Requires substellar cooling models. |
| Planet | Mass, composition, age, irradiation, atmospheric assumptions; radius and temperature depend on those | Unsupported; future Earth/Jupiter masses/radii, K | No generator. Neither stellar IMF nor stellar homology applies. Moon is a possible orbital relationship, not a separate intrinsic material kind. |
| Smaller body | Mass or size plus density/composition, albedo, rotation, irradiation | Unsupported; future kg, km, kg/m³, K | No generator or collisional size distribution. Material strength and fragmentation would need separate modelling. |
| Simplified merger remnant | Retained mass after enhanced capture or physical contact; anchored radius/luminosity homology determines T consistently | Any positive retained scene mass, deliberately uncapped; M☉, R☉, K, L☉ | Runtime mergers only. No table extrapolation and no claim of a detailed physical remnant type or lifespan. |

The current scene gives stars an enlarged, mass-scaled capture radius as an explicit artistic exception to physical-surface-only merging; the physical radius and stellar attributes remain unchanged until a merger.

All supported bodies also have position (pc), velocity (converted to km/s when needed), stable identity and survivor relationships. An added physical kind must define its own collision surface or capture rule, source data, supported parameter domain and rendering interpretation. Only future specialist models can establish their distributions and limits; the unsupported rows are not promises of universal astronomical boundaries.
